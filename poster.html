2How it works

Every pipe weld in the workshop is still made by hand, and a manual weld depends on the person holding the torch. Meanwhile customers keep asking for cleaner welds, welds that come out the same every time, and proof of how each one was made.

The head clamps onto the tube and carries the torch right around the joint, on its own. The welder sets the programme, then watches it run.
One turn, four sectors, four sets of settings: flat, vertical down, overhead, vertical up. Sector by sector, the molten pool never gets the chance to run.

3What would change

A bead that comes out the same every time The pulsed current holds the pool, whatever the position.
Roughly half the welding time On the joint taken as a reference, times still to be measured.
Every weld recorded by the machine, not by hand The traceability customers now ask for comes with the process.

Nothing in the standards stands in the way. The welding procedure is qualified first, then the operators to ISO 14732, then the usual inspections. The road is well marked out.
